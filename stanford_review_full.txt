Questions for Authors
1. Can you provide a public code/model release (including ONNX/TRT exports
and switch_to_deploy scripts) and exact inference pipelines used for latency
measurements, so the community can reproduce the reported timings and
FPS/J metrics?
2. How exactly is the “Harmonized Hat-Only Protocol” constructed per dataset
(mapping rules, ignored classes, IOU thresholds, NMS, confidence thresholds)?
Could you share a standardized evaluation harness to facilitate fair cross-
dataset comparisons?
3. DABFNet reports mAP50=94.9 and mAP50–95=62.6 on SHWD; can you include
DABFNet in your main comparison table with matched image size/engines and
add a runtime comparison, to contextualize Rep-YOLO11s against a closely
performing PPE-specific baseline?

minimal fine-tuning (e.g., 1–5% labeled data) or domain adaptation, and if so,

Overall Assessment
This is a solid applied engineering paper that thoughtfully integrates known
components with a clear deployment focus and provides unusually thorough
latency audits across devices and engines. The technical soundness is good, the
design decisions are well-motivated by a roofline analysis, and the exportability of
sparse attention without custom plugins is a pragmatic contribution. However, the
empirical accuracy gains over a strong YOLO11s baseline are modest on SHWD,
key ablation runs are left incomplete, and cross-domain results are mixed despite
the claim of robust transfer. The novelty is moderate, as the method primarily
combines existing ideas with careful placement rather than introducing new
algorithms. For a top-tier applied AI/vision track, I view this as borderline: it
provides real value for deployability and reproducibility in an important domain,
but it would benefit from stronger comparative baselines (e.g., DABFNet in the
main table), fully completed ablations, standardized cross-dataset evaluation, and
a public release of code/models. I lean toward a weak reject in the current form,
with a path to acceptance if the above issues are addressed.
