Summary
The paper proposes Rep-YOLO11s, a hardware-aware, real-time detector
specialized for safety helmet compliance monitoring on construction sites. The
method integrates structural re-parameterization (RepConv) to reduce memory
access costs at inference, injects a lightweight Stem CoordConv for spatial priors,

with Task-Aligned Assignment (TAL) to address geometric quality and severe class
imbalance. On SHWD, the model achieves 94.83% mAP50 and 62.54% mAP50-95

Questions for Authors
1. Can you provide per-scale (APs/APm/APl) and per-class (helmet vs person)
breakdowns to quantify tiny-object gains more directly, and include
confidence intervals for key metrics on the SHWD test set?
2. How exactly was the “Harmonized Hat-Only Protocol” implemented for prior
works (e.g., EC-YOLOv8)? Were those models retrained/re-evaluated under
your pipeline, or were numbers adapted from publications? Please release the
evaluation scripts and mapping rules.
3. Could you report cross-domain baselines (e.g., YOLOv8s, YOLO11s baseline) on
GDUT-HWD, Hard Hat Workers, and SHD to contextualize your zero-shot
transfer improvements?
4. How robust is the TRT export of BiFormer across different TensorRT versions
and dynamic-shape settings? Have you profiled worst-case latency variance for
dynamic TopK/Gather under dense scenes?
5. What is the ablation of Focal EIoU vs CIoU/GIoU specifically on small helmets
(APs) and on crowded occlusions? Is TAL essential once Focal EIoU is used, or
are their contributions additive?
6. Could you share INT8 calibration results (accuracy/latency/throughput) for
edge deployment and any sensitivity to calibration set size/content?
7. Beyond the bipartite spatial filter heuristic (helmet–person overlap), have you
considered lightweight person–part or pose priors to reduce false alarms
without harming recall?
Overall Assessment
Limitations include modest accuracy lifts over a strong baseline and
dependence on NVIDIA-centric tooling; broader backend coverage (e.g.,
INT8 calibration, OpenVINO, ARM NPUs) would increase generality.

This is a strong applied paper that thoughtfully integrates hardware-aware
structural re-parameterization, selective attention, and positional priors to a
practically important surveillance task. The deployment-centric evaluation—
covering TensorRT exportability, multi-GPU latency, energy efficiency, and end-to-
end pipeline timing—is a notable strength that distinguishes the work from many
academic treatments. The primary weaknesses are limited methodological
originality (careful integration of known components rather than a new paradigm),
small headline accuracy gains over a robust YOLO11s baseline on the principal
two-class test protocol, and incomplete cross-domain contextualization against
baselines. Nevertheless, the paper is well-written, technically sound, and delivers
actionable insights and artifacts for real-world PPE compliance systems. With
clarifications on harmonized evaluation, more granular per-class/scale metrics,
and added cross-domain baseline comparisons, the contribution would be
compelling for the AAIML Applied AI and Computer Vision Track.
