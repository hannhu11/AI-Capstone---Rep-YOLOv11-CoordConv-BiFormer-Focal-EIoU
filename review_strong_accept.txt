Methodological novelty is primarily in combining known components
(RepConv, CoordConv, BiFormer, Focal-EIoU, TAL) rather than introducing

•
The approach remains NMS-based with DFL; it does not explore more
recent NMS-free, quantization-friendly heads (e.g., YOLO26/RT-DETR
variants) that could be competitive in both latency and tiny-object recall.
•
In Table II (multi-seed, standardized SGD), the full system (A6)
underperforms the baseline (A0) by ~1.2 mAP50 and ~1.24 mAP50-95 on
average, contradicting the single-run AdamW “benchmark” improvement
and raising concerns about robustness of the claimed accuracy gains.
•
Cross-paper accuracy and FPS comparisons mix different training recipes,
hardware, and precision modes; several baselines are taken from
literature rather than re-trained under a matched protocol and runtime.
•
The claimed per-class gains (notably for “hat”) are not consistently
reconciled with joint mAP drops for “person,” and the paper does not
report confidence intervals or statistical tests for these per-class
outcomes.
•
No Jetson or embedded NPU results are reported (despite “embedded
devices” claims); MX230/CPU are helpful but not representative of typical
modern edge AI accelerators used on sites.
•
The optimizer switch (AdamW for “benchmark” vs SGD for multi-seed)

stating the direction of effect, which, judging by the means, appears
negative for A6 vs A0.

Overall Assessment
This is a well-executed applied paper with a clear deployment focus, sensible
architectural choices, and extensive latency profiling. The integration of RepConv
(neck-only), CoordConv stem, and BiFormer for small-object focus is coherent and
well-motivated for helmet detection. The cross-domain HHW zero-shot
improvements over a YOLO11s baseline are promising, and the systems-level
details (ONNX/TensorRT compatibility, pipeline timing) make the work practically
useful.
However, the central accuracy claims on SHWD are insufficiently substantiated:
under standardized multi-seed SGD, the full system underperforms the baseline,
while the single-run AdamW benchmark shows a slight improvement. This
inconsistency, the reliance on mixed, non-matched baselines from the literature,
and the absence of comparisons to modern NMS-free detectors (YOLO26/RT-
DETR) weaken the evidence that the proposed combination offers a consistent
accuracy–latency advance over strong contemporary alternatives. Methodological
novelty is modest, as the paper primarily composes existing techniques.
