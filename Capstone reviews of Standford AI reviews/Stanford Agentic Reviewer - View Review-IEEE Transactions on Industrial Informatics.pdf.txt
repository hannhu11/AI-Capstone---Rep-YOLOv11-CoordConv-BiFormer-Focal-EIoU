Summary
The paper proposes Rep-YOLO11s, a real-time helmet detector for construction
surveillance 
that 
integrates 
four 
known 
components: 
structural 
re-
parameterization (RepConv) to fuse multi-branch training blocks into single 3×3
convolutions at inference, CoordConv to inject positional priors, BiFormer bi-level
routing attention to emphasize salient tokens, and Focal EIoU for bounding-box
regression. On SHWD, the authors report mAP50 up to 94.83% (single run) and
96.64% (5-fold mean), strong throughput on GPUs (2.92–5.35 ms FP16 TensorRT at
640×640), and zero-shot transfer to multiple external datasets. They also provide
detailed deployment measurements and an end-to-end RTSP pipeline analysis.

Experimental gaps or methodological issues
Clarity or presentation issues
Missing related work or comparisons
Conceptual novelty is limited: all core components (RepConv, CoordConv,
BiFormer, Focal EIoU) are established; the contribution is primarily an
integration/tailoring to YOLO11s.
•
The claim that Focal EIoU resolves severe class imbalance is conceptually
incorrect; it is a regression loss and does not address class-imbalance in
classification directly.
•
The impact of BiFormer at inference is under-quantified; attention
modules add compute and memory access, and cannot be “re-
parameterized away” like RepConv.
•
Metric inconsistencies across tables are concerning (e.g., mAP50–95 on
SHWD reported as 62.54% in Table I vs 77.90% in Table III without clear
protocol differences), calling into question result comparability.
•
SOTA comparisons mix measurement conditions; it is unclear if all
latencies were measured under identical engines/hardware and if models
were re-trained under matched protocols.
•
EC-YOLOv8 is reported with much higher mAP50–95 (74.6%) than the
proposed method (62.54%), yet the narrative claims superiority “in both
accuracy and throughput,” which is not supported by the table.
•
The ablation gains are small and sometimes within noise; no
variance/confidence intervals are reported for ablations.
•
Multiple typographical inconsistencies (YOLO1ls vs YOLO11s; “EloU” vs
EIoU) and occasional contradictory statements (e.g., saliency analysis on
posters) reduce clarity.
•
Lack of precise BiFormer hyperparameters (region size S, top-k) and
insertion points; unclear where CoordConv is injected and at what cost
across layers.
•
Important related/backbone work like YOLOv6/EfficientRep (rep-friendly
backbones and necks for TensorRT/T4) and recent helmet detectors (e.g.,
DABFNet) or reparameterized necks (e.g., MAF-YOLO) are not compared
experimentally; this diminishes the context for claimed speed/accuracy
trade-offs.

Overall Assessment
This manuscript targets a timely and important industrial application and
demonstrates commendable attention to real-world deployment details, including
synchronized GPU timing and end-to-end pipeline profiling. The integration of
rep-style blocks, CoordConv, and BiFormer is reasonable for small-object
detection in cluttered scenes, and the extensive cross-domain evaluation is a
welcome attempt to assess robustness. However, the work’s methodological
novelty is modest, and several critical issues limit its suitability in its current form:
internal inconsistencies in reported metrics, potentially unfair SOTA comparisons,
over-claims 
(e.g., 
about 
class-imbalance 
mitigation), 
and 
incomplete
quantification of attention overhead at inference. The ablation gains are small and
lack statistical support, and key related baselines (e.g., YOLOv6/EfficientRep,
recent helmet detectors) are not compared under a unified protocol. With a
thorough cleanup of evaluation inconsistencies, standardized and fair baseline
comparisons, precise reporting of attention hyperparameters/overhead, and
added edge-device validation, this work could provide solid practical value. As
submitted, I recommend a major revision.
