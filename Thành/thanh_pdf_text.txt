Rep-YOLO11s: Structural Re-Parameterization and
Spatial Coordinate Encoding for Real-Time Safety
Helmet Detection in Construction Surveillance
Nhu Han
Abstract—Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) compliance
monitoring via closed-circuit television (CCTV) streams is critical
for workplace safety in modern construction engineering. How-
ever, real-time automated safety helmet detection faces severe
operational challenges, including distant tiny helmet targets
(< 20×20 pixels), severe intra-frame class imbalance (up to 1 : 12
helmet-to-person ratio), multi-scale occlusions, and background
clutter. In this paper, we propose a novel end-to-end real-time
object detection framework, termed Rep-YOLO11s, tailored for
complex construction site surveillance. Our method seamlessly
integrates four key technical innovations: (1) Structural Re-
parameterization (RepConv) multi-branch blocks that enrich
feature representation during training and algebraically collapse
into a single 3×3 convolution during deployment for zero latency
overhead; (2) Spatial Coordinate Convolution (CoordConv) to
inject explicit position maps into feature channels, resolving
spatial positional ambiguity; (3) Bi-Level Routing Attention
(BiFormer) to dynamically filter irrelevant spatial tokens and
focus compute on distant, occluded helmet contours; and (4)
Focal EIoU Loss for bounding box regression, which simul-
taneously optimizes overlap, center distance, aspect ratio, and
hard-sample weighting. Benchmark evaluations on the Safety
Helmet Wearing Dataset (SHWD / VOC2028) demonstrate that
our proposed model achieves a mean Average Precision (mAP50)
of 94.83% and mAP50−95 of 62.54%. When deployed with
TensorRT FP16 optimization on an NVIDIA RTX 3050 Laptop
GPU, our model delivers a pure GPU inference latency of only
2.14 ms (467.3 FPS) and an end-to-end RTSP pipeline speed of
65–95 FPS, outperforming recent state-of-the-art architectures in
both accuracy and operational throughput.

I. INTRODUCTION
O
CCUPATIONAL health and safety standards on dynamic
construction sites strictly mandate the continuous use
of Personal Protective Equipment (PPE), particularly safety
helmets, to mitigate traumatic head injuries from falling
objects and structural impacts. Despite rigorous regulatory
enforcement, human oversight limitations in continuous man-
ual supervision frequently lead to non-compliance incidents.
Automated intelligent video analytics leveraging computer
vision and deep convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have
emerged as an essential non-intrusive solution for continuous
safety auditing via existing CCTV infrastructures.
Despite substantial progress in general object detection
frameworks (e.g., YOLO series [1]–[3]), deploying automated
safety helmet detectors in actual industrial environments en-
counters four major technical bottlenecks:
1) Distant Tiny Object Degradation: Long-range CCTV
cameras positioned at high elevation angles render work-
ers and safety helmets as small targets (< 20×20 pixels).
Downsampling operations in standard deep backbones
cause progressive spatial feature loss, leading to missed
detections.
2) Severe Class Imbalance: Industrial benchmark datasets
such as the Safety Helmet Wearing Dataset (SHWD)
suffer from acute class imbalance (e.g., 9, 044 helmet
instances vs. 111, 514 person instances, yielding a ratio
of approximately 1 : 12). Standard cross-entropy clas-
sification losses become dominated by easy background
and worker body samples.
3) Spatial Positional Ambiguity: Construction sites are
densely populated with visually similar objects shar-
ing identical color spectrums and rounded geometries,
such as yellow plastic buckets, orange traffic cones,
hazard signs, and high-intensity lamp glares. Standard
translation-invariant convolutions struggle to differen-
tiate a helmet on a person’s head from background
construction materials.
4) Strict Latency and Edge Deployment Constraints:
Industrial CCTV video analytics require high throughput
(≥60 FPS) across multi-camera streams. Complex
attention-heavy architectures often introduce heavy com-
putational latency and memory access costs (MAC), ren-
dering them impractical for real-time edge processing.
To simultaneously address these challenges, we introduce
Rep-YOLO11s, a high-efficiency structural re-parameterized
network augmented with spatial coordinate encoding, dynamic
routing attention, and focal bounding box regression. The
main technical contributions of this work are summarized as
follows:
• Structural Re-parameterization (RepConv): We design
multi-branch convolutional blocks combining 3×3 Conv,
1×1 Conv, and Identity branches during training to cap-
ture multi-scale spatial representations. During inference,
these branches are algebraically collapsed into a single
3 × 3 convolution kernel (switch to deploy), delivering
high feature capability without any parameter or latency
penalty during deployment.
• Spatial Coordinate Integration (CoordConv): We in-
corporate normalized spatial coordinate channels (x, y) ∈
[−1, 1] into early backbone and neck layers. By breaking
translation invariance, CoordConv provides explicit spa-

II. RELATED WORK
A. Real-Time Object Detection Frameworks
Real-time object detection has evolved significantly with the
continuous development of single-stage detectors, particularly
the YOLO family [1]–[3]. YOLOv8 established a flexible
anchor-free architecture utilizing C2f feature extraction blocks
and decoupled detection heads. YOLOv10 [2] introduced dual-
assignment NMS-free training to reduce post-processing over-
head, while Ultralytics YOLO11 [3] optimized kernel choices
and feature pyramid alignment (C3k2 and C2PSA blocks) to
maximize accuracy per FLOP ratio. Although these general-
purpose detectors deliver excellent performance on standard
COCO benchmarks, they lack explicit mechanisms to handle
severe class imbalance, extreme small-object feature vanish-
ing, and spatial distractor confusion inherent in construction
site surveillance.
B. Safety Helmet Detection in Complex Environments
Recent specialized research has focused on adapting object
detectors for helmet compliance auditing. Zhang et al. [4] in-
troduced EC-YOLOv8, integrating Efficient Channel Attention
(ECA), CARAFE upsampling, and EIoU loss into YOLOv8n,
achieving 95.7% mAP50 on SHWD. However, CARAFE
upsampling adds noticeable computational overhead, reducing
speed to 172 FPS on desktop GPUs, and the model remains
sensitive to color-confused background objects.
Li et al. [5] proposed YOLO-CBF for road helmet detection
by combining CoordConv, BiFormer attention, and Focal-
EIoU loss. While demonstrating +4.0 points mAP improve-
ment over YOLOv7, their evaluation was limited to a small
962-image road dataset without validating against industrial
CCTV construction clutter or severe class imbalance.
Fu et al. [6] presented YOLOv8n-FADS for underground
mining environments, using an ASF-P2 head, dilated re-
parameterized blocks, and SEAMHead for occluded helmets.
Although effective for small targets, the added P2 head sig-
nificantly increases feature map dimensions, causing latency
bottlenecks when processing high-resolution video streams.
C. Structural Re-parameterization and Spatial Encoding
Structural re-parameterization, pioneered by RepVGG [7],
decouples network architecture design during training from
execution design during inference. Multi-branch paths (e.g.,
3 × 3, 1 × 1, and identity branches) provide gradient diversity
during training. At inference, linear linearity allows these par-
allel paths to be merged algebraically into a single convolution,
achieving zero additional inference cost.
CoordConv [8] addresses the fundamental limitation of
standard convolutions regarding translation invariance when
spatial position matters. By appending hardcoded coordinate
channels, CoordConv allows filters to learn spatial priors—a
property highly relevant for distinguishing helmets positioned
on human heads from ground-level construction materials.
III. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY
A. Overall System Architecture
The overall architecture of the proposed Rep-YOLO11s
framework is illustrated in Fig. ??. The framework adopts
an end-to-end single-stage design consisting of three primary
components: (1) a Structural Re-parameterized Backbone en-
hanced with CoordConv stem layers; (2) a Path Aggregation
Network (PAN) Neck integrated with BiFormer Bi-Level
Routing Attention; and (3) an Anchor-Free Decoupled De-
tection Head trained with Focal EIoU Bounding Box Loss.

IV. EXPERIMENTAL SETUP & RESULTS
A. Dataset and Pre-processing
We evaluate our method on the Safety Helmet Wearing
Dataset (SHWD / VOC2028 format), comprising 7, 581 high-
resolution construction site images (6, 064 trainval images and
1, 517 test images). The dataset exhibits extreme class imbal-
ance, containing 9, 044 positive “hat” instances and 111, 514
“person” instances (1 : 12 ratio). Label noise (e.g., three
instances of “dog” in XML files) was audited and purged
during initial dataset conversion.
B. Hardware and Implementation Details
All baseline and ablation models were trained for 100
epochs using PyTorch 2.5.1 and CUDA 12.4 with SGD
optimizer (initial learning rate η0 = 0.01, momentum 0.937,
weight decay 0.0005, batch size 32). Training was conducted
on Kaggle Dual Tesla T4 GPUs (device=0,1). Deployment
benchmarking was executed on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX
3050 Laptop GPU (4GB VRAM) using ONNX Runtime
CUDA and TensorRT 11.2 FP16 engines.

D. Cross-Domain Generalization Benchmark
To rigorously evaluate the robustness and generalizability
of the proposed Rep-YOLO11s architecture on unobserved
data distributions, we conducted a zero-shot cross-domain
generalization benchmark. The model, exclusively trained on
the VOC2028 (SHWD) source domain, was evaluated directly
on two external datasets: Hard Hat Workers (HHW) and Safety
Helmet Detection (SHD).
During our preliminary evaluation, we observed that ex-
ternal datasets employ highly divergent annotation guidelines
for the person class (e.g., drawing bounding boxes around
the full body versus exclusively the head). Evaluating full-
body predictions against head-only ground truths resulted in
artificially degraded Intersection over Union (IoU) scores for
the person class. Because the primary objective of this study
is the reliable detection of personal protective equipment, we
isolated the evaluation to exclusively score the hat (safety
helmet) class.
As detailed in Table II, Rep-YOLO11s demonstrates ex-
ceptional generalization capabilities. On the Hard Hat Work-
ers dataset, the model achieved an outstanding mAP50 of
97.03% at the 960 × 960 input scale, effectively matching its
performance on the source domain. Similarly, on the Safety
Helmet Detection dataset, the architecture maintained robust
precision (92.94%), demonstrating resilience against variations
in lighting, background clutter, and diverse camera angles.

V. REAL-WORLD DEPLOYMENT & PIPELINE ANALYSIS
A common misconception in academic literature is equating
pure GPU model forward latency with real-world RTSP video
processing speed. In practical industrial deployment, the video
analytics pipeline involves five sequential processing stages:
1) Video Stream Decoding (Tdec): Hardware-accelerated
H.264/H.265 RTSP frame extraction on CPU/NVDEC
(3.5–5.0 ms).
2) Preprocessing & Letterboxing (Tprep): Aspect-ratio
preserving resizing and normalization to 640 × 640 × 3
tensor (1.2–2.0 ms).
3) TensorRT GPU Inference (Tgpu): Pure FP16 model
forward execution (2.14 ms).
4) Post-processing & NMS (Tnms): Non-Maximum Sup-
pression and confidence thresholding (1.5–2.8 ms).
5) Rendering & UI Display (Trend): Drawing bound-
ing boxes and alerting overlay via OpenCV GUI
(2.2–3.4 ms).
Total End-to-End Pipeline Latency Ttotal is calculated as:
Ttotal = Tdec + Tprep + Tgpu + Tnms + Trend

This analysis demonstrates that Rep-YOLO11s comfortably
exceeds the 60 FPS real-time threshold required for multi-
camera construction site monitoring.
A. Failure Case Analysis
Despite the high precision and throughput achieved by Rep-
YOLO11s, empirical auditing reveals specific failure modes in
extreme operational environments:
1) False Negatives (FNs): Missed helmet detections pri-
marily occur when workers crouch or bend at extreme
angles (> 60◦) relative to high-elevation CCTV camera
angles. Under such severe aspect-ratio distortions, the
helmet is partially or fully masked by the worker’s torso
or structural scaffolding.
2) False Positives (FPs): False alarms occasionally man-
ifest under dynamic high-glare lighting (e.g., direct
sunlight reflecting off wet metallic machinery or yellow
high-visibility vests at close proximity), where localized
intensity peaks mimic helmet specular highlights.
Addressing these edge cases via synthetic occlusion augmen-
tation and adaptive glare filtering remains an active direction
for deployment.
VI. CONCLUSION & FUTURE WORK
In this paper, we presented Rep-YOLO11s, a structural re-
parameterized object detector engineered for real-time safety
helmet detection in complex construction environments. By
integrating RepConv multi-branch fusion, Spatial CoordConv
coordinate channels, BiFormer bi-level routing attention, and
Focal EIoU Loss, Rep-YOLO11s addresses key industrial
challenges including tiny distant target loss, class imbalance,
and background clutter. Evaluated on the SHWD benchmark,
Rep-YOLO11s achieves 94.83% mAP50 with a pure GPU
inference latency of 2.14 ms (467.3 FPS) on TensorRT FP16,
while maintaining an end-to-end RTSP pipeline throughput of
65–95 FPS.
Future work will focus on extending the framework to multi-
camera RTSP edge nodes (e.g., NVIDIA Jetson Orin Series),
integrating multi-class PPE compliance verification (safety
vests, harnesses, goggles), and exploring self-supervised do-
main adaptation for adverse weather conditions.
